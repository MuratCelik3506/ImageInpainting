In recent years, the field of image inpainting has attracted significant attention in the domains of computer vision and graphics. Numerous inpainting models have been proposed and developed by researchers and practitioners, aiming to seamlessly fill missing or damaged areas in images with pixels that blend harmoniously with the surrounding context. In this paper, our objective is to evaluate and compare the performance of various interior staining models using a standardized dataset, thereby contributing to the advancement of computer vision and graphics.

Inpainting models employ a diverse range of techniques and algorithms to achieve their goals. Deep learning approaches have been leveraged in some models, utilizing contextual attention or attention mechanisms to capture intricate details and structures. Additionally, generative models, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), have been utilized to produce realistic and diverse interior painting results. The primary objective across all these models is to achieve high-quality inpainting outcomes with seamless transitions between the painted area and the rest of the image.

To conduct our experiments, we have carefully curated a comprehensive datasets that encompasses various challenges associated with inpainting. These datasets comprises different masks, allowing for the painting of specific areas within the image, as well as masks randomly marked at certain percentages. By applying the tested inpainting models to these datasets, we can quantitatively and qualitatively evaluate their performance, enabling a comprehensive comparison of their strengths and limitations.

The evaluation of interior staining results relies on performance evaluation criteria, which measure the quality of the outcomes. These criteria include visual similarity measures, such as the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), as well as statistical measures like the L1 and L2 losses. Through the analysis of these metrics and the comparison of different models' performances, we can gain insights into the respective strengths and weaknesses of each approach.

In summary, this paper focuses on the evaluation and comparison of various inpainting models using  standardized datasets. By conducting these experiments, we aim to contribute to the understanding of inpainting techniques and applications, ultimately fostering advancements in the fields of computer vision and graphics.
